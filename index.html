Equilibrium Flow Maps

Paper · CMU · under review at ICLR 2027 · co-first author

Flow maps make flow models fast by jumping along the flow. Equilibrium models drop the time input and let samples settle on the data, which makes refinement natural but takes infinitely long. Can one model do both?

  • We introduce a flow map for equilibrium models: one network moves a sample along the flow by any duration, from a short refinement step to a jump straight to the data.
  • The key step: compressing the infinite time line onto the interval from 0 to 1, so that "jump to the end" becomes a duration of 1.
  • On ImageNet, FID 2.90 with one call and 1.96 with eight (lower is better).

Neural finite volume solver

Paper · BAIR, UC Berkeley · co-first author, with A. M. Bayen et al.

Conservation laws describe traffic, fluids and waves, and their solutions develop sharp jumps, like the back of a traffic jam. Can a neural network solve them accurately without breaking conservation?

  • We learn only the flux, the flow between neighboring cells, inside a classical finite volume solver. What leaves one cell enters the next, so the solution stays conservative.
  • At comparable cost it often beats standard solvers such as Godunov's scheme and WENO, with an error about 8× lower than Godunov's in our best setting.
  • It trains from solved examples or from the equation alone, and we prove it can reach any accuracy on a fine enough grid.

Anti-bubbles

IPT 2024 · Problem 15

The problemAn antibubble is water trapped in water, inside a thin shell of air. How do they form? How large and how long-lived can you make them, and are size and lifetime related?

We made both the largest (19 mm across) and the longest-living (1 h 11 min) antibubbles we know of!

Alcohol sound check

IPT 2024 · Problem 4

The problemSome Brazilian distilleries judge their spirit by the sound of the bottle: tapped right after being turned upside down, it gives a short, dull sound instead of ringing. What causes this? Why does it fail with carbonated drinks, and which liquids keep the effect longest?

The tiny bubbles made by the flip cause it. Surprisingly, the sound is damped by heat: its vibration squeezes each bubble, heating it locally, and that energy is lost!

Smart Water

IPT 2024 · Problem 7

The problemIf you let water flow in a maze, you get a physical maze-solving algorithm. Many other algorithms and computations can be done with physical systems. Create a water-based computer, without electronics, that solves an NP-hard problem.

Our soap films solve the Steiner tree problem (the shortest network joining given points), sometimes faster than the best algorithms on regular hardware!

Pool Vortex Rings

IPT 2024 · Problem 12

The problemDrag a plate through still water and two black circles appear on the bottom of the pool: the shadows of whirlpools at the surface. From a short video of the shadows, can you predict how long the whirlpools will last?

From the problem's pool video, we predicted about 11 minutes. In our tank, the same method predicted 14 ± 4 s and we measured 11 ± 1 s!

The Galton Board

IPT 2024 · Problem 1

The problemBeads dropped through rows of evenly spaced pegs are said to pile up in a bell shape (a binomial distribution). Can changing the pegs, their layout or the beads give other shapes? Can independent, identical beads escape the central limit theorem? What if the board vibrates?

Place the pegs at the right points and a board can produce any pile shape you choose: we simulated bell-shaped, log-normal and exponential ones!
